A keeper uses a real pick, so those slots are gone before the draft starts. Value compares the pick it costs against where that player normally goes — positive means you're getting him later than the market does.

Value = the pick a keeper costs, minus that player's ESPN PPR average draft position. Christian Watson at pick 61 against a 94.5 ADP is −34: he'd likely have been there 33 picks later. It's a market comparison, not a prediction — ADP is where people draft, not how players finish.

Which keepers actually gain the most

The Value column in the table above just measures how far down the board you're getting someone. A late-round keeper always scores well there, because everything looks like a bargain at pick 93 — it says nothing about whether the player is actually any good.

So this ranks something more useful: how many more points a keeper gives you than the best player you could have drafted with that same pick. Loveland has the best Value score on the board, but he ranks third here, because a tight end who beats his replacement by 37 points is worth less than Rashee Rice beating his by 65.

Every player is measured against replacement level at his own position — QB 292, WR 196, RB 193, TE 169 — because raw points don't compare across positions in a one-QB league. Kickers and defenses are left out; they're streamed, not drafted. Projections are ESPN's.

Keeper rules

All nine · what a keeper costs · the two-year clock

Complete redraft, but you may keep one player from last season, who then uses one of your draft picks. Keeping is optional — one player or none, no penalty for passing.

What it costs
went undrafted → round 7
drafted by anyone → that round − 1
  1. 1

    On your roster

    The player has to already be on your roster.

  2. 2

    Round 4 or later

    He has to have been drafted in the 4th round or later — or gone undrafted entirely.

  3. 3

    One round earlier

    A keeper uses a pick one round earlier than where he was drafted last season. Keep a 4th-rounder and he becomes your 3rd-round pick.

  4. 4

    Undrafted costs a 7th

    If he went undrafted that season, keeping him costs your 7th-round pick. If anyone drafted him, his cost comes off that round instead — even if you got him off waivers, free agency or a trade.

    Updated Aug 2026. The old wording read as though any waiver pickup paid a flat 7th, which made a late-round wire find more expensive rather than less.

  5. 5

    Second year, one more round

    Keep the same player two years running and he costs two rounds earlier than his original draft round. A 4th-rounder goes to your 3rd, then your 2nd.

  6. 6

    Two years maximum

    Two seasons is the limit on any one player — and the clock follows the manager, not the player. If someone else already used their two years on him, you can pick him up and get your own two.

  7. 7

    Injury swap allowed

    If your keeper gets hurt you can swap him for another player off your roster. The replacement is priced on his own history, so a swap can cost more than what it replaces. The injured player doesn't burn a year.

  8. 8

    Nobody gets dropped

    You don't have to drop anyone. Tell KillaTae17 who you want and he sets it in the draft.

  9. 9

    The deadline

    Keeper picks are due before the deadline — Saturday August 29th 2026 at 7:00 PM Pacific this season.
